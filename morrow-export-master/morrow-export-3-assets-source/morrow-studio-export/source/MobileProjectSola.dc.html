(Overview)

A two-person ceramics studio in Stroud making stoneware for restaurants, shops and its own store. It needed an identity that holds up on a menu credit and in a shop window.

Every shoot is art-directed around use — plates mid-service, bowls with food in them — never pieces alone on seamless white.

Fig. 01–02 — Celadon vase; breakfast bowl

Every piece is different. The brand shouldn’t be.

(Packaging)

Stamped by hand, posted without plastic.

Moulded pulp inserts in four standard sizes, inside a plain box stamped in iron-oxide ink. A rubber stamp costs less than one print run.

(Gallery)Swipe — 03

(Wholesale)

A one-page trade sheet with lead times, minimums and glaze samples. Stockists get the same photography, so the work looks consistent wherever it’s sold.

Outcome: stockists grew from [X] to [Y] in the year after launch.

“Chefs now send us photos of our plates on their pass. That’s the brand doing its job.”

— Co-founder, Sola Ceramics
